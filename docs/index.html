What is the task?

The task is an online version of the prospective memory (or PM) sentences task (Morys-Carter & Fredman Stein 2016, used in Fredman Stein et al. 2018). the ongoing task is rating 90 sentences 1–7 for valence (1 very negative, 7 very positive, 4 neutral). hidden among them are 6 target sentences about food or vehicles (random per participant), first around trial 10–14, then every ~10–14 trials. when a target appears, the participant presses `\` or `T` as well as rating it. runs about 10 minutes: ID entry, instructions, a 3-question check, 6 practice trials, a pause after trial 45, CSV download at the end. nothing uploads.

It studies event-based prospective memory; the intention (press `\` or `T`) has to be remembered and carried out when the cue appears, while the person is busy with the rating task. it measures PM hits (of 6), false alarms (pressing on non-targets), PM response time, plus ongoing-task accuracy and speed, so you can see both PM failures and how much the extra intention costs the main task. sentence valence varies, so it can also test whether PM differs for negative vs positive vs neutral material. the original study used it to show rumination impairs PM cue detection and slows PM responses to negative sentences.
